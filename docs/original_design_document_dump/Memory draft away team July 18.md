\#\# SECTION 1: CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY & GAME OVERVIEW  
\#\#\# 1.1 The Semi-Cooperative Paradox  
\*\*Away Team\*\* is built upon a fundamental psychological conflict: \*\*collaborative survival versus individual supremacy\*\*. Players do not win or lose as a team; victory is personal, tracked strictly through individual Faction Victory Points (VPs). However, absolute failure is collective. If the agency collapses due to structural decay, roster extinction, or administrative ruin, the game terminates immediately, handing a hollow, unearned victory to whoever happens to be leading a compromised scoreboard.  
This framework forces a constant tactical friction known as the \*\*Tragedy of the Commons\*\*:  
 \* \*\*The Shared Burden:\*\* Factions must cooperate during field deployments. Stages feature high Difficulty Ratings that require coordinated dice commitments, tactical items, and specialized class abilities. Neglecting a teammate's crisis or withholding support causes stages to fail, which rapidly advances the global \*\*Doom Tracker\*\* or inflicts physical trauma across the entire active roster.  
 \* \*\*The Greedy Incentive:\*\* Every resource spent to preserve the team—be it Faction Credits funded into base upgrades, tokens burned to shield a rival’s asset, or precious static dice exhausted to clear a failing room—is an asset directly deducted from that player's private endgame scoring potential.  
The dominant strategy is not pure altruism, nor is it pure sabotage. The path to victory requires riding the absolute razor's edge of structural negligence: contributing just enough resource volume to keep the agency operational, while forcing your political rivals to bleed out their own vaults to cover the difference.  
\#\#\# 1.2 The Skewed Tag Pyramid  
To eliminate the common design trap of "diluted rosters"—where highly specialized characters sit around with no relevant tasks during long stretches of gameplay—\*Away Team\* operates on an intentionally weighted, asymmetric content distribution. The entire campaign ecosystem, from core storyline operations to randomized side missions, is structured around a strict hierarchy of operational challenges.

\#\#\#\# Tier 1: The Baseline (Combat — \~50%)  
Half of all challenges encountered in the field require tactical violence. \*\*\[Combat\]\*\* is the structural backbone of the micro-loop. Because hostile environments are fundamentally dangerous, every single agent on the roster—regardless of their primary discipline—understands how to fire a weapon. This ensures that no character is ever dead weight; every faction can actively pitch in to clear a firefight.  
\#\#\#\# Tier 2: The Operational Objective (Intel, Infiltrate, Negotiate — \~35%)  
These tags represent \*how\* a mission is actually completed, bypassed, or politically leveraged. While Combat represents the operational tax you pay to survive a room, Tier 2 tags are the critical chokepoints where missions are won or lost. They require precise specialist intervention; throwing raw, un-labeled support characters into these rooms is wildly inefficient.  
\#\#\#\# Tier 3: The Friction Hazards (Operations, Survival — \~15%)  
The rarest cards in the deck, acting as sudden environmental gear-checks or technical hurdles (e.g., terminal hacking, toxic fallout runs). They exist to disrupt perfect planning, force sudden tactical retreats, or pressure factions into spending liquid Credits mid-mission to bypass complex mechanical blockades.  
\#\#\# 1.3 The Static Pool Paradigm  
In standard tactical board games, dice represent a recurring action economy that refreshes completely at the start of every turn or room. \*Away Team\* entirely shatters this safety net by introducing the \*\*Static Pool Paradigm\*\*.  
When a character deploys from the Home Base roster bench, their starting dice pool (ranging from 3 to 5 physical dice based on their class profile and modifications) is a \*\*finite, non-refreshing currency for the entire mission duration\*\*.

\#\#\#\# The Mechanical Gravity of Exhaustion  
When a player commits dice to resolve a stage, those physical components are exhausted and placed aside. They are \*\*not\*\* reclaimed when the team moves to the next sequential Stage Card. Some highly specific character abilities, faction perks, or consumable armory items can restore exhausted dice, but these are rare, expensive exceptions.  
This economic reality transforms the act of rolling into a high-stakes calculation. Committing a massive pool of dice guarantees a Phase 1 Specialist victory and isolates your agent from Backfire hazards, but it leaves that character completely defenseless and spent for the remainder of the operation. Factions must constantly balance their expenditure, choosing exactly when to dominate a room, when to coast on the backs of their rivals, and when to execute a tactical retreat to preserve their physical assets for the final endpoint.

\#\# SECTION 2: FACTION ARCHITECTURE & PLAYER SETUP  
\#\#\# 2.1 Faction Vaults & Resource Pooling  
In \*Away Team\*, players do not operate as independent field agents; they embody institutional \*\*Factions\*\* competing for political dominance within Agency Headquarters. Consequently, individual characters do not possess private wealth, equipment inventories, or ability tokens. All operational assets are centralized within the physical \*\*Faction Vault\*\* managed on each player’s dual-layer Faction Dashboard.  
\#\#\#\# The Pooling Protocol  
 \* \*\*Liquid Assets:\*\* All Credits and universal Tokens earned as mission rewards or stage bounties are deposited directly into the Faction Vault's shared track.  
 \* \*\*The Armory Cache:\*\* Acquired item cards (Weapons, Armor, Specialty Devices, and Consumables) belong strictly to the Faction, not the character who recovered them. When sitting on the Home Base bench, all characters are stripped bare of gear.  
 \* \*\*The Deployment Loadout:\*\* During the Base Phase, when preparing for a mission drop, a Faction distributes assets from their pooled Vault onto the sheets of the characters they are actively deploying. Universal tokens can be allocated to characters (up to a hard maximum cap of 5 tokens per character) to serve as fuel for their unique Class and Label active abilities.  
 \* \*\*The Safe Return:\*\* Upon successful completion of a mission, all surviving characters return to HQ. Their equipped items, unspent credits, and remaining tokens are automatically unequipped and funneled straight back into the Faction Vault.  
\#\#\# 2.2 Political Influence Power (PIP)  
\*\*Political Influence Power (PIP)\*\* is a non-VP, secondary executive currency tracking a Faction's bureaucratic leverage, backroom blackmail, and institutional sway over Agency Command. While Credits buy tactical gear and fund physical facilities, PIP commands the administrative trajectory of the entire campaign.  
\#\#\#\# 1\. PIP Generation Matrix  
A Faction accumulates PIP through deliberate operational alignment and supreme institutional sacrifice:  
 \* \*\*Institutional Alignment:\*\* Earn \*\*+1 PIP\*\* during the Aftermath Phase if your Faction successfully satisfied a face-up Open Agenda card mid-mission.  
 \* \*\*The Bureaucrat's Cut:\*\* Earn \*\*+1 PIP\*\* during the HQ Phase whenever your Faction contributes the single largest majority of Credits to fund a Base Facility Upgrade.  
 \* \*\*Sacrifice for the Agency:\*\* When a character owned by your Faction suffers permanent death in the line of duty, Agency Command honors their ultimate sacrifice. Your Faction immediately gains \*\*+2 PIP\*\* on your Faction Dashboard.  
\#\#\#\# 2\. Administrative Expenditures  
During the Base and HQ Phases, PIP is spent to dictate agency-wide decisions, override rival choices, or absorb the operational fallout of field salvage:  
 \* \*\*Operation Selection Voting:\*\* When choosing which active Side Operation card the squad will deploy to next, players participate in a secret fist-bid using PIP. The highest bidder selects the destination. All spent PIP is discarded to the bank.  
 \* \*\*Directing Infrastructure:\*\* When a Base Facility Upgrade is fully funded, the player who contributes the single largest volume of PIP to the project determines exactly \*which\* facility upgrade tile is slotted into the board, regardless of who contributed the most Credits.  
 \* \*\*The Bureaucratic Veto:\*\* A player may spend \*\*2 PIP\*\* at any point during the Base Phase to completely freeze a base facility's passive perk for one full round, blocking a rival player from utilizing its benefits.  
 \* \*\*The Vulture Tax:\*\* When a character owned by an ally dies mid-mission, a rival player may step in to loot the dropped gear. Doing so forces the looting player to immediately deduct \*\*1 PIP\*\* from their Faction Dashboard for every 2 dropped items they claim (rounded up).

\#\#\# 2.4 Faction Customization Components  
To ensure complete asymmetry every time the campaign is initialized, a Faction's baseline identity is modified by three distinct card-driven components.  
\#\#\#\# 1\. The Faction Perk Deck  
At the conclusion of setup, each player is dealt one random card from the \*\*Faction Perk Deck\*\*. Perks represent permanent, passive sub-class structural modifications unique to your faction for the entire campaign.  
 \* \*Strategic Utility:\* Drawing a Combat-focused perk can shore up the weaknesses of a technocratic group like the Prometheus Initiative, while drawing a financial perk can turn the Ironclad Syndicate into an absolute economic monopoly.  
\#\#\#\# 2\. Open Agendas  
Each Faction maintains a face-up hand of exactly \*\*2 Open Agenda cards\*\*, drawn randomly from a shared pool during setup. Open Agendas represent short-term, public tactical mandates issued by Agency Command (e.g., \*Clear an Operations stage using zero items\*).  
 \* \*The Loop:\* The moment a faction satisfies the listed conditions mid-mission, they instantly claim the listed VP and PIP rewards, discard the card, and immediately draw a face-up replacement from the deck. Open Agendas provide an active, dynamic path for rapid mid-game scoring.  
\#\#\#\# 3\. Secret Objectives  
During setup, each player is secretly dealt exactly \*\*1 Secret Objective card\*\* from a massive, randomized pool containing dozens of unique options. This card remains hidden face-down on the player’s dashboard for the entire campaign.  
 \* \*The Long Game:\* Secret Objectives dictate hidden ideological agendas that award massive blocks of VPs during the final end-game accounting based on specific, often ruthless playstyles (e.g., \*Ensure that at least two Scientist characters owned by rival players end the campaign inside the Dead Pool\*). Because there are far more Secret Objectives than players, opponents can never be entirely certain what hidden metrics their rivals are plotting toward in the shadows.

\#\# SECTION 3: GROUP SCALING & DEPLOYMENT RULES  
\#\#\# 3.1 The 4-Character Deployment Mandate  
Regardless of the human player count sitting around the table, every tactical mission in \*Away Team\* enforces a strict, unyielding squad size of exactly \*\*4 characters\*\*. The design architecture of the micro-loop (stage difficulty, dice attrition math, and defensive support requirements) is entirely balanced around this 4-character baseline.  
Before a mission drop can occur, the team must collectively satisfy two operational criteria listed on the selected Mission Briefing Card:  
 \* \*\*Composition Gating:\*\* Missions feature strict keyword requirements driven by character \*\*Labels (Tags)\*\*. A mission card might read: \*"Requires \\ge 2 \[Combat\] labels and \\ge 1 \[Intel\] label across the squad."\* The 4 selected characters must collectively possess these labels to legally launch the drop ship.  
 \* \*\*Deployment Recommendations:\*\* The mission card displays an optimized target metric for total health pools and collective starting dice volumes (e.g., \*Recommended Collective Dice Pool: 16+\*). While these are not hard legal gates like labels, they serve as a analytical warning system for factions planning their deployments.  
\#\#\# 3.2 Scaling Architecture by Player Count  
To maintain the exact same tactical tension across different group sizes, character ownership is divided into permanent, isolated personal stashes assigned directly to human players. Characters are never shared or pulled from a common pool during deployment.

\#\#\#\# Solo Mode (The Attrition Sandbox)  
A single player manages all 4 deployment slots and controls the entire roster bench. All faction-to-faction political rules, bidding wars, slot extortion, and trading mechanics are deactivated. The game scales into a pure, strategic survival calculation where the player pits their collective roster against the system's crushing dice attrition engine.  
\#\#\#\# 2-Player Configuration (The Dual-Specialist Front)  
Each player commands their own independent Faction. To fulfill the 4-character mandate, \*\*each player must deploy exactly 2 characters\*\* from their personal roster bench to the mission squad. Players manage separate Faction Vaults, split rewards based on which of their characters cleared specific stages, and can openly trade items and credits between their two active agents.  
\#\#\#\# 4-Player Configuration (The True Specialist Ownership)  
The purest competitive format. Each of the 4 players controls a distinct Faction and \*\*deploys exactly 1 character\*\* to the squad. Individual agency is absolute; you have total tactical ownership over one specialized body on the ground, making mid-mission table talk, binding contracts, and mutual protection intensely personal.  
\#\#\# 3.3 The 3-Player Matrix: The Vanguard Slot  
When exactly 3 players are competing, the player-to-character ratio introduces an intentional structural asymmetry. To bridge the gap to the 4-character mandate, the game introduces the \*\*Unassigned Vanguard Slot\*\*.  
\#\#\#\# 1\. The Setup Architecture  
Each of the 3 players must maintain a personal roster bench of at least \*\*2 active characters\*\*. At the start of the Base Phase, each player automatically assigns exactly 1 character from their personal stash to fill deployment slots 1, 2, and 3\. Slot 4 remains completely vacant.  
\#\#\#\# 2\. The Pre-Mission Vanguard Auction  
Before equipment loadouts are finalized or the drop ship launches, the 3 players participate in an immediate \*\*Vanguard Auction\*\* to win operational control of Slot 4:  
 \* \*\*The Bidding:\*\* Factions openly bid \*\*Credits\*\* directly from their pooled Faction Vaults. Bidding passes clockwise starting with the player holding the highest baseline Political Influence Power (PIP).  
 \* \*\*The Currency Lock:\*\* The highest bidder deducts the promised Credits from their Faction Vault and claims Slot 4\.  
\#\#\#\# 3\. Double-Deployment Mechanics  
The winning Faction selects a \*\*second character\*\* from their personal roster bench and inserts them into the mission squad. This double deployment alters the competitive dynamic of the entire mission:  
 \* \*\*Increased Resource Burn:\*\* The double-deploying faction now burns through their static faction dice pool at twice the speed of their opponents, heavily exposing their roster to exhaustion.  
 \* \*\*High-Volume Harvesting:\*\* Conversely, the double-deploying player now commands two harvesting agents on the ground. They have twice the opportunity to claim the \*Closer Bonus (+1 Loyalty)\*, score mid-mission Open Agendas, and overwhelmingly dominate the \*\*Dice Attrition Bonus (+2 Loyalty)\*\* during the Aftermath Phase.  
\#\#\# 3.4 The Mandatory Deployment Mandate  
Because \*Away Team\* is a highly competitive game where an early game-over (Operational Collapse) triggers immediate point tallying, trailing players might identify a tactical advantage in committing institutional sabotage. To prevent an underdog from intentionally withholding their agents to trigger a collapse and lock down the current scoreboard, the game enforces the \*\*Mandatory Deployment Mandate\*\*.  
\> \#\#\# 📋 Administrative Directive: Mandatory Deployment  
\> If a Faction possesses at least one healthy, active character card on their personal bench that legally satisfies the mission's Label Composition Gating, \*\*that Faction is legally barred from opting out of the mission.\*\* They must fill their designated deployment slot.  
\>   
\#\#\#\# Legal Operational Alternates  
If a player desperately desires to protect a high-VP veteran asset from entering a dangerous high-tier mission, they cannot simply choose to sit out. They must execute one of two legal workarounds during the Base Phase:  
 1\. \*\*Negotiated Spot-Trading:\*\* The player can approach a rival and openly sell their deployment spot on the mission. The rival pays Player Credits, items, or direct VP transfers to double-deploy a character into that slot. The selling player benches their veteran safely, collects the profits, and surrenders their deployment rights for that drop.  
 2\. \*\*Administrative Leverage:\*\* The player can spend \*\*Political Influence Power (PIP)\*\* during the mission selection step to aggressively out-bid their opponents and force a base-wide \*Rest & Recovery\* phase, locking down the drop ship for everyone.

\#\# SECTION 4: THE MISSION PHASE (THE TACTICAL MICRO-LOOP)  
\#\#\# 4.1 Anatomy of a Stage Card  
The tactical core of \*Away Team\* is driven by the \*\*Stage Card Deck\*\*, a linear sequence of encounters compiled during the Base Phase according to the selected Mission Briefing. Each Stage Card represents a distinct physical room, security perimeter, or operational crisis.  
Every card contains five structural fields that dictate its mechanical behavior:  
 \* \*\*Stage Type (Label/Tag):\*\* Defines the primary nature of the challenge (\[Combat\], \[Intel\], \[Infiltrate\], \[Negotiate\], \[Operations\], or \[Survival\]). This tag maps directly to character competencies, determining which agents act as Specialists or Support.  
 \* \*\*Difficulty Rating:\*\* An absolute integer value representing the total number of successes the squad must collectively generate to clear the card (e.g., \*Difficulty: 5\*).  
 \* \*\*The Stage Modifier Slot:\*\* A dedicated space where a card from the \*\*Stage Modifier Deck\*\* is drawn face-up during the stage briefing. These modifiers layer unpredictable variables—such as localized environmental hazards, technical blackouts, or corporate buyouts—onto the baseline stage math.  
 \* \*\*Clearance Reward:\*\* The standard bounty of Faction Credits, universal Tokens, or character Loyalty points awarded to the squad immediately upon a successful resolution.  
 \* \*\*Failure Penalty:\*\* The structural, chronological, or physical consequence inflicted upon the squad or the agency if the collective success threshold is not met.  
\#\#\# 4.2 The Dynamic Initiative Order  
When a new Stage Card and its corresponding Stage Modifier are revealed, the squad enters an explicit, multi-tiered resolution sequence designed to let players evaluate threat levels before spending their finite, static dice pools.  
\#\#\#\# Step 1: The Briefing & Transaction Window  
Factions analyze the revealed tags, difficulty, and modifier text. Before any dice are physically rolled, players may freely trade Credits and items between their active characters' equipment slots, formulate binding tactical contracts, or spend Credits to pay off specific environmental buyout costs listed on the modifier card.  
\#\#\#\# Step 2: Phase 1 Initiative (The Specialists)  
Characters who possess the revealed stage’s keyword as a \*\*Primary Label\*\* are granted immediate tactical priority.  
 1\. \*\*Commitment:\*\* Specialist players openly negotiate and decide exactly how many unexhausted dice to commit from their character sheets to the active pool.  
 2\. \*\*The Specialist Roll:\*\* All committed Specialist dice are rolled simultaneously.  
   \* \*Success:\* Each die resulting in a \*\*3+\*\* adds \+1 success toward the Difficulty Rating.  
   \* \*Backfire:\* Rolling a natural \*\*1\*\* results in 1 point of immediate, un-preventable physical damage to that specific character.  
 3\. \*\*Early Resolution Check:\*\* If the total successes generated by the Specialists meet or exceed the Difficulty Rating, the stage is instantly \*\*Resolved with Success\*\*. The sequence skips directly to Step 5\. Any uncommitted dice on the remaining Support characters are safely preserved on their sheets, entirely bypassing their risk windows.  
\#\#\#\# Step 3: Phase 2 Initiative (The Support)  
If the Specialists fail to clear the target difficulty, the threat remains active. The remaining deployed characters (those with the keyword as a Secondary Label, or those with no relation to the label at all) must step up to cover the success deficit.  
 1\. \*\*Commitment:\*\* Support players evaluate the missing success margin and decide how many remaining dice to commit from their static pools.  
 2\. \*\*The Support Roll:\*\* All committed Support dice are rolled simultaneously. Success and Backfire thresholds are determined by the agent's exact tag relation (see \*4.3 Standardized Support Risk\*).  
\#\#\#\# Step 4: Post-Initiative Window  
Certain character unique powers, custom weapon modifications, or passive Faction perks trigger exclusively \*after\* all baseline initiative dice have been rolled. Factions may spend universal tokens or activate specific abilities now to convert failure faces into successes, trigger rerolls, or absorb incoming damage.  
\#\#\#\# Step 5: Aftermath & Attrition Tally  
The total success pool is compared against the Difficulty Rating:  
 \* \*Success:\* The stage is cleared. The squad claims the listed Clearance Rewards, funneling Credits and Tokens directly into their respective Faction Vaults.  
 \* \*Overkill:\* If the success total exceeds the difficulty by 2 or more, an Overkill victory is achieved, triggering a specialized payout from the Overkill Matrix.  
 \* \*Failure:\* The stage fails. The squad suffers the immediate Failure Penalty listed on the card, and the mission moves to the next sequential Stage Card unless a critical failure explicitly aborts the operation.  
\#\#\# 4.3 Standardized Support Risk Math  
To ensure that stepping out of a primary discipline does not mathematically penalize players with a higher frequency of physical trauma, the physical danger threshold is perfectly standardized across all phases of execution. \*\*A character can only take Backfire damage on a natural roll of a 1, regardless of their labels.\*\*  
Instead, operating as a non-specialist is heavily penalized through \*\*extreme success inefficiency\*\*, draining the Faction's static dice pools for a lower success probability.

\#\#\#\# The Tactical Retreat Option  
Because rolling unmatched dice during Phase 2 is an incredibly inefficient use of a finite resource, factions are never forced to roll their remaining dice to save a room. During the transition into Step 3 (Phase 2 Initiative), the active squad can collectively vote to execute a \*\*Tactical Retreat\*\*:  
 \* \*The Cost:\* The active stage card is immediately marked as Failed, and the squad sustains the full text of the Failure Penalty.  
 \* \*The Benefit:\* The phase ends instantly before any Support dice are rolled. Factions cleanly preserve their remaining static dice pools and health tracks, allowing them to save their strength for subsequent rooms where they hold primary specialist dominance.  
\#\#\# 4.4 The Overkill Matrix  
When the active squad coordinates efficiently and exceeds a Stage Card’s Difficulty Rating by \*\*2 or more successes\*\*, they break the encounter's threat threshold and trigger an \*\*Overkill Victory\*\*. In addition to standard clearance rewards, a tag-specific economic windfall is immediately pulled from the bank and deposited directly into the participating Factions' Vaults:  
 \* \*\*🟥 \[Combat\] — The Spoils of War:\*\* Heavy tactical dominance yields immediate resource scavenging. Add \*\*+2 Credits\*\* directly into the pooled Faction Vaults of every player who committed at least one die to the stage.  
 \* \*\*🟥 \[Survival\] — Hardened Veterans:\*\* Overcoming extreme physical attrition breeds legendary status. Grant \*\*+1 Loyalty\*\* point to every single character deployed on the active mission squad.  
 \* \*\*🟦 \[Intel\] — Data Bonanza:\*\* Complete decryption siphons valuable telemetry. Add \*\*+1 Token\*\* to the vaults of all participating factions, and award an extra \*\*+1 Credit\*\* to the specific Faction whose character rolled the final success face (The Closer).  
 \* \*\*🟦 \[Operations\] — System Cache:\*\* Overclocking terminal architecture unlocks hardware reserves. The squad immediately draws \*\*1 random Consumable Item card\*\* from the base armory deck straight into the shared squad inventory.  
 \* \*\*🟩 \[Negotiate\] — Political Favors:\*\* Absolute psychological dominance yields bureaucratic leverage. Grant \*\*+1 Loyalty\*\* point exclusively to the characters who successfully rolled a success face during the resolution.  
 \* \*\*🟩 \[Infiltrate\] — Clean Run:\*\* Bypassing detection parameters leaves local asset vaults perfectly ripe. Award \*\*+2 Credits\*\* straight to the Faction whose character threw the highest volume of natural 6s during the initiative rolls.

Credits and Tokens can change hands instantly to fuel emergency mid-mission arms deals, allowing wealthy factions to fund cash-strapped combatants in exchange for high-tier firepower.

\#\#\#\# 1\. Progression Operations (Story Missions)  
Progression Operations are fixed, high-narrative encounters printed directly onto the Master Campaign Sheet. They represent critical milestones in the campaign storyline (e.g., \*The Obsidian Vault\* or \*Citadel Apex\*).  
 \* \*\*Access Gates:\*\* These operations are permanently locked at the start of an Act. Factions cannot deploy to them until the team has collectively accumulated a designated threshold of \*\*Progress Points\*\* via side missions.  
 \* \*\*High-Stakes Fallout:\*\* Completing a Progression Operation advances the global narrative to the next Act, unlocks higher-tier items in the Shared Armory marketplace, and opens premium tiers of the Recruitment Pile. Side Operations are modular, volatile missions drawn from a randomized Mission Deck during the Base Phase. They represent the day-to-day work of the agency: resource raids, data extraction, and tracking anomalous signatures.  
 \* \*\*The Resource Engine:\*\* Side Operations are the primary economic vehicle for Factions. They are explicitly run to harvest Credits, siphoning universal Tokens, and build up character Loyalty before tackling the brutal story operations.  
 \* \*\*The Threat Grid:\*\* The Mission Deck always displays two active Side Operation cards face-up at HQ. At the end of every HQ Phase, any unselected Side Operation is discarded into the archive and replaced with a fresh card from the deck. Some Side Operations feature time-sensitive warning tags; ignoring a "Critical Threat" card and letting it cycle out of the market automatically advances the global Doom Tracker by \+1.

\# AWAY TEAM: OPERATIONAL MANUAL  
Welcome to the Agency, Director.  
\*\*Away Team\*\* is a tactical game of survival and political backstabbing for 1 to 4 players. Together, you must manage elite agents and deploy them into deadly hot zones to stop global threats.  
But here is the catch: \*\*you are not a team.\*\* You are rival \*\*Factions\*\* competing for executive control of Agency Headquarters. You must work together just enough to keep the Agency from collapsing, while ensuring your rivals bleed out their resources so your Faction walks away with the ultimate victory.  
\#\# 📦 THE CORE CONCEPTS  
Before you crack open your first mission dossier, you must understand the two golden rules of the field.  
\#\#\# 1\. Static Dice Pools (The Attrition Clock)  
When your agent steps off the drop ship, the dice on their card are all they have. If an agent starts a 3-stage mission with 5 dice, \*\*those dice do not refresh between rooms.\*\* If you roll 3 dice to clear the first hallway, you only have 2 dice left for the rest of the entire mission. Spend them carefully.  
\#\#\# 2\. The Danger Pyramid (Everyone Fights)  
Hostile territory is incredibly violent. Roughly \*\*50% of the encounters\*\* you face will be \*\*\[Combat\]\*\* stages. Because of this, every agent in the game knows how to handle a rifle. Your choice isn't \*if\* an agent can fight, but \*what else\* they bring to the table when the bullets stop flying (like Science, Stealth, or Diplomacy).  
\#\# 🏛️ SETUP: YOUR FACTION BOARD  
Every player chooses a Faction Dashboard (e.g., \*The Ironclad Syndicate\* or \*The Ghost Network\*).  
Your Faction Board acts as your private vault. \*\*Characters do not own money or gear; your Faction does.\*\*  
 \* \*\*The Vault:\*\* Place your starting Credits, universal Tokens, and Faction Perk card here.  
 \* \*\*The Roster:\*\* Put your starting character cards on your bench.  
 \* \*\*Political Influence Power (PIP):\*\* This is your tracking currency for votes, vetoes, and underhanded deals.  
\#\# 🪖 THE MISSION PHASE  
A game consists of several missions. Each mission displays a recommended squad layout and an \*\*Operations Track\*\*—a visible line of Stage Cards you must clear in order.  
Every mission requires a squad of exactly \*\*4 characters\*\*. In a 4-player game, everyone sends 1 agent. In a 2-player game, you each send 2\. (In a 3-player game, the vacant 4th slot is auctioned off to the highest bidder for Credits).  
\#\#\# Anatomy of a Stage Card  
When you enter a room, flip the Stage Card and draw a card from the \*\*Stage Modifier Deck\*\* to add a random hazard or opportunity to the room. Look at the card's \*\*Tag\*\* (e.g., \[Combat\], \[Intel\], \[Infiltrate\]) and its \*\*Difficulty\*\* (the number of successes needed to clear the room).  
\#\#\# The Turn Sequence  
Every single room is resolved using a strict 4-step initiative ladder:  
\#\#\#\# 1\. Phase 1: The Specialists Act First  
Any deployed character who has the room's Tag as a \*\*Primary Label\*\* goes first.  
 \* Specialist players negotiate and decide how many dice to throw from their remaining pools.  
 \* \*\*The Specialist Roll:\*\* Roll the dice. Every \*\*3+\*\* is a success. Every natural \*\*1\*\* causes \*\*1 point of immediate Backfire damage\*\* to that character.  
 \* \*Early Clearance:\* If the Specialists generate enough successes to meet the Difficulty, the room is cleared instantly\! The rest of the squad saves their dice, taking zero risk.  
\#\#\#\# 2\. Phase 2: The Support Steps Up  
If the Specialists fail to clear the room, the remaining characters must roll to cover the deficit. Your success threshold depends on your agent's relationship to the tag:  
 \* \*\*Secondary Label:\*\* You score successes on a \*\*4+\*\*. You take damage on a natural \*\*1\*\*.  
 \* \*\*No Label (Unmatched):\*\* You score successes on a \*\*5+\*\*. You take damage on a natural \*\*1\*\*.  
\> ⚠️ \*\*Tactical Retreat:\*\* If the Support characters don't want to risk throwing their precious remaining dice on a bad 5+ gamble, the squad can collectively vote to \*\*Concede the Stage\*\*. The room fails immediately and inflicts its penalty, but you preserve your dice pools for the next room.  
\>   
\#\#\#\# 3\. Post-Initiative  
Players may spend universal Tokens, activate character unique abilities, or use weapon modifications to flip failures into successes or heal damage.  
\#\#\#\# 4\. Room Resolution  
 \* \*\*Success:\*\* Collect the room’s Credits and Tokens, depositing them into your Faction Vaults.  
 \* \*\*Overkill:\*\* If you beat the difficulty by 2 or more, claim a bonus reward based on the room's tag (e.g., bonus cash for Combat, free items for Operations).  
 \* \*\*Failure:\*\* Suffer the penalty on the card and advance the global \*\*Doom Tracker\*\*.  
\#\# 💀 DEATH, LOOTING, AND THE DEAD POOL  
When an agent hits \*\*0 Hit Points\*\*, they are on the brink of death.  
\#\#\# 1\. Emergency Stabilization  
Any player at the table may immediately spend \*\*1 Token\*\* or discard \*\*1 Medkit\*\* to stabilize the dying agent. If stabilized, they reset to 1 HP and keep fighting. If no one pays, the agent is \*\*Killed\*\*.  
\#\#\# 2\. Stripping the Corpse (The Vulture Economy)  
When an agent dies, all of their equipped items drop onto the current Stage Card.  
 \* \*\*Return Home:\*\* The squad can agree to leave the gear in the cargo bay. It returns to the original owner's vault for free at the end of the mission.  
 \* \*\*The Pillage:\*\* Any rival player can aggressively steal the dead agent's gear for their own vault. However, they must pay a \*\*Vulture Tax: \-1 PIP for every 2 items they steal.\*\* If multiple players want the same gun, they roll off with a d6.  
\#\#\# 3\. The Dead Pool Ledger  
Place your dead character cards face-up in your \*\*Dead Pool\*\*. Death is not a game-over. At the end of the campaign, your martyrs are celebrated: every point of Loyalty on a dead card converts directly into \*\*Victory Points (1 Loyalty \= 2 VPs)\*\*.  
\#\# 🏢 THE HQ PHASE (DOWNTIME)  
Between missions, you return to Agency Headquarters to rebuild. Factions vote on how to spend their downtime.  
\#\#\# Choice A: Deploy Immediately  
You head straight to the next mission brief. Wounded characters must immediately roll on the brutal \*\*Home Base Injury Chart\*\*, risking permanent trauma or forced Convalescence (sitting out the next mission) because they didn't get bed rest.  
\#\#\# Choice B: Rest & Recovery (Lockdown)  
The agency pauses to heal. Advance the global \*\*Doom Tracker by \+1\*\* and draw a random \*\*Command Event Card\*\*. In exchange, \*\*every benched agent automatically heals 2 HP\*\* and all Convalescence timers are cleared.  
\#\#\# Funding Base Upgrades  
During downtime, you can spend Credits to build modules like the \*Trauma Ward\* or \*Prototype Foundry\*.  
 \* \*\*Money Into Points:\*\* Every \*\*1 Credit\*\* you contribute to a building project instantly rewards your Faction with \*\*+1 VP\*\*.  
 \* \*\*Lead Architect:\*\* The player who contributes the most cash to a building wins a massive \*\*+3 to \+5 VP bonus\*\* and gets to choose exactly which facility upgrade tile is slotted into the base.  
\#\# 🏆 WINNING THE CAMPAIGN  
The game tracks progress across a master campaign file. You will run randomized \*\*Side Operations\*\* to gather gear and cash, using them to unlock the high-stakes \*\*Progression Missions\*\* that advance the story arc.  
\#\#\# Collective Defeat  
If the global \*\*Doom Tracker hits 6\*\*, the base is compromised. The game ends in a collective Agency failure, and the scoreboard is locked.  
\#\#\# Individual Victory  
If the squad successfully completes the final Progression Mission on the campaign sheet, the world is saved\! Players reveal their hidden \*\*Secret Objective Cards\*\* and add up their total Victory Points from:  
 \* Accumulated VPs during play.  
 \* Loyalty ranks on living and dead characters.  
 \* Completed Secret Objectives.  
The Faction leader with the highest score takes total executive command of the Agency.
